Undergraduate Thesis: Development of an interpretable ensemble model to predict immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy response in bladder cancer patients

2025

Authors: Sadia Ruhama*, Syed Ayman Shahbad*, Ahnaf Warid (*equal contribution)

Developed an ensemble feature selection framework combining SVM-RFE, RF-RFE, Mutual Information, and LR-RFE with frequency-based majority voting to identify predictive biomarkers for pembrolizumab response in bladder cancer. Applied to the GSE111636 dataset (11 patients, 70,000+ gene features) using nested Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation to address the "curse of dimensionality" in low-sample, high-dimensional gene expression data. Benchmarked against deep learning approaches (Concrete Autoencoder, L1-regularized Neural Network), the ensemble method identified a stable 11-gene candidate biomarker signature (including TOP2A, ZEB2, PTCH1, CDCA5), validated through DAVID functional enrichment analysis linking the genes to immune checkpoint regulation and tumor progression pathways.

Keywords: Bladder Cancer · Ensemble Feature Selection · Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor (ICI) Therapy · High-Dimensional Gene Expression · Interpretable Machine Learning

An Exploratory Multiome Analysis of a BCG-Associated HSPC Signature: Trained-Immunity Marker in Blood and an Adverse Prognostic Association in Tumour

2026

Author: Sadia Ruhama

Conducted an exploratory integrative analysis of paired scRNA-seq and scATAC-seq data from 653 hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs) across two bladder cancer patients before and after BCG treatment. Applied MOFA+ to identify a candidate BCG-associated latent factor and derive gene signatures, including 48 genes shared across patient-specific and joint models. Investigated regulatory motifs and biological pathways, and evaluated signature associations across four external datasets, revealing potentially context-dependent patterns in immune cells and bladder cancer outcomes.

Rare Cell-Type Identification Under Batch Shift: Bayesian Active Learning on a LoRA-Adapted Vision Foundation Model

2026

Author: Sadia Ruhama

Developed an uncertainty-aware active learning framework for rare cell-category recognition in microscopy images under simulated extreme class imbalance and limited labeled data. Integrated DINOv2, parameter-efficient LoRA fine-tuning, Bayesian uncertainty estimation (MC-Dropout), and class-balanced BALD to prioritize informative samples for annotation. Evaluated on LIVECell using held-out imaging wells, achieving a macro-F1 of 0.629 and rare-class AUPRC of 0.627, demonstrating the framework’s potential for label-efficient cell recognition under imaging batch variation.

Keywords: Vision Foundation Models · Biomedical Image Analysis · Bayesian Active Learning · Rare Cell Classification · Uncertainty-Aware Learning

The Trusted Assistant Blind Spot: Human Detection of and Compliance with Environmentally-Injected Attacks on VLM-Mediated Augmented Reality

2026

Authors: Tuhin Mridha, Sadia Ruhama, Himika Tasnim

A human-centered cybersecurity research project investigating how environmental prompt-injection attacks on Vision-Language Model (VLM)-powered Augmented Reality (AR) assistants influence human decision-making. The study proposes controlled experiments comparing unaided, uncompromised, and adversarially manipulated AR guidance to examine attack detectability, behavioral compliance, and failure attribution. Using behavioral measurements, participant interviews, and mixed-effects logistic regression, the research aims to understand how trust in AI, task complexity, and malicious payload presentation influence user responses to compromised guidance, highlighting human-centered vulnerabilities in AI-assisted environments.

Keywords: Human–AI Interaction · VLM · Augmented Reality · Prompt Injection · AI Security

Advancement in Detection of Melanoma: A Systematic Literature Review on CNN Utilization

2024

Authors: Sadia Ruhama, Anit Paul

Conducted a Systematic Literature Review (SLR) using a PRISMA-based selection process, initially retrieving 109,275 records from six digital libraries and selecting 18 studies on CNN-based melanoma detection. Examined how dataset size, diversity, model architectures, and optimization techniques influence classification performance. Reviewed findings included an improvement in Inception-v3's F1 score from 54.10% to 96.29% with data augmentation and Bayesian tuning. The review highlighted the potential of CNNs to support melanoma image classification while identifying limitations involving data availability, interpretability, computational requirements, and overfitting.

Keywords: Melanoma Detection · Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) · Systematic Literature Review (SLR) · Skin Cancer · Medical Image Classification · Deep Learning · Computer-Aided Diagnosis
